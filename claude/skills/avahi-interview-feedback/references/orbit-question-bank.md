# ORBIT Interview Question Bank (from Avahi People Operations, Confidential)

Ownership, Results (Deliver Results), Band Member, Innovation, Trust (Earn Trust). One STAR worksheet per selected question. Questions marked (Manager) are for people manager candidates.

## O: Ownership

**Definition:** Leaders are owners. They think long-term and don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results. They act on behalf of the entire company, beyond just their own team. They never say "that's not my job."

**IC looks like:** asks questions and considers future outcomes (scalable, long-term value); gives feedback and coaches and develops peers; speaks up in meetings, questions and challenges respectfully; understands their role and relationship with other roles; partners with peers across the organization.
**Manager looks like:** creates a vision for the team aligned to the customer experience; fosters autonomy; thinks about the impact of decisions on other teams and the customer over time; coaches the team on the big picture.

Questions:
- Tell me about a time when you took on something significant outside your area of responsibility. Why was it important? What was the outcome?
- Give me an example of a time when you didn't think you were going to meet the commitments you promised. How did you identify the risk and communicate it to stakeholders? What was the outcome?
- Tell me about a time you made a hard decision to sacrifice short-term gain for a longer-term goal.
- Give an example of when you saw a peer struggling and decided to step in and help. What was the situation and what actions did you take? What was the outcome?
- What steps do you take to ensure projects you complete get transitioned effectively to new owners? Give an example where you elected to re-engage on a project you had already transitioned.
- How do you ensure your team stays connected to the company vision and the bigger picture? Give an example of when you felt a team or individual goal was in conflict with the company vision. What did you do? (Manager)
- Tell me about an initiative you undertook because you saw it could benefit the whole company or your customers, but wasn't within any group's individual responsibility. (Manager)

## R: Deliver Results

**Definition:** Leaders focus on the key inputs for their business and deliver them with the right quality and in a timely fashion. Despite setbacks, they rise to the occasion and never settle.

**IC looks like:** focuses on the most important tasks while adapting as needed; perseveres through setbacks and overcomes obstacles.
**Manager looks like:** keeps the customer in focus; sets and communicates smart team goals, expectations and priorities; keeps people focused yet nimble; removes barriers; celebrates success while keeping focus on the right results.

Questions:
- Tell me about a time you (and your team) were driving toward a goal and were more than halfway to the objective when you realized it may not be the best goal or may have had unintended consequences. What was the situation and what did you do?
- Tell me about a goal that took a long time to achieve, or that you are still working toward. How do you keep focused given competing priorities?
- Tell me about a time where you not only met a goal but considerably exceeded expectations. How were you able to do it? What challenges did you overcome?
- Give me an example of a time when you delivered an important project under a tight deadline. What sacrifices did you make? How did they impact the final deliverables?
- Tell me about a time you had significant, unanticipated obstacles to overcome in achieving a key goal. Were you eventually successful?
- How do you ensure you are focusing on the right deliverables when you have several competing priorities? Tell me about a time when something fell through the cracks. (Manager)
- What's your approach to setting stretch goals for your team that are challenging yet achievable? Tell me about a time you didn't hit the right balance. How did you adjust? (Manager)
- Give an example of a mission or goal you didn't think was achievable. How did you help your team try to achieve it? Were you successful? (Manager)

## B: Band Member

**Definition:** Being a collaborative team player who elevates the group over individual recognition, working selflessly as part of the ensemble rather than seeking the spotlight as a solo performer.

**IC looks like:** gives credit to teammates publicly even when they played a significant role; volunteers to support others when their own plate is full; puts team goals ahead of personal recognition; speaks up for colleagues' contributions in meetings and with leadership; avoids behaviors that undermine team cohesion.
**Manager looks like:** celebrates team wins over individual wins; gives credit to team members and direct reports in front of leadership; steps in to support other teams even with no direct benefit; models selfless behavior and calls it out.

Questions:
- Tell me about a time you worked on a team where you could have taken individual credit for an outcome but chose to share it with the group. Why did you make that choice? What was the impact?
- Describe a situation where a colleague or team was struggling and you stepped in to help, even though it wasn't your responsibility. What did you do? What was the outcome?
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a team decision but committed to it anyway and supported the group's direction. How did you handle it? What was the result?
- Give me an example of a time you actively promoted a teammate's work or idea to leadership or a broader audience. What motivated you? What was the outcome?
- Tell me about a time when team dynamics were off (conflict, competition, or low trust between members). What role did you play? What did you do to improve the situation?
- Tell me about a time you had a team member who was competing with peers rather than collaborating. How did you identify it? What did you do? What was the outcome? (Manager)
- How do you build a culture where your team celebrates collective wins over individual ones? Give a specific example of how you reinforced that behavior. (Manager)

## I: Innovation

**Definition:** Challenging the status quo and continuously learning new skills to bring fresh thinking and improved approaches to your work.

**IC looks like:** proactively seeks new skills, tools or approaches relevant to the role; questions assumptions about how work gets done and proposes better ways; acts on fresh ideas rather than only identifying that change is needed.
**Manager looks like:** challenges existing processes and encourages the team to question why things are done the way they are; creates space for new ideas without defaulting to "this is how we've always done it"; develops own skills and brings learning back to the team.

Questions:
- Tell me about a time you challenged an established process or approach in your work. What made you question it? What did you change, and what was the outcome?
- Describe a skill or area of knowledge you identified as a gap and proactively worked to develop. How did you go about learning it? How did it change the way you work?
- Tell me about a time you brought a fresh approach to a recurring problem that others had accepted as just the way things work. What did you do differently?
- Give me an example of a time you learned something new (a tool, methodology, or industry development) and applied it to improve how you or your team worked.
- Tell me about a time you proposed a change or new idea that was met with resistance. Why did you believe in it? How did you handle the pushback? What was the result?
- Describe a situation where you noticed industry trends or external developments and brought fresh thinking into how you approach your work. How did you stay current?
- Give me an example of a time you identified that your team's approach had become stale or outdated. How did you surface it and what did you do to drive a different way of working? (Manager)
- How do you create an environment where your team feels safe to challenge the status quo and try new approaches? Give a specific example. (Manager)
- Tell me about a time you invested in developing a team member's skills or exposed them to new thinking that changed how they approached their work. (Manager)

## T: Earn Trust

**Definition:** Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing so is awkward or embarrassing. They benchmark themselves and their teams against the best.

**IC looks like:** does what they say they will do, or appropriately resets expectations; honors commitments made to other teams even if their own goals are in jeopardy.
**Manager looks like:** earns trust by consistently making good decisions, keeping commitments and adhering to high ethical standards; provides an environment where people can take smart risks and learn from mistakes; listens, communicates and delegates.

Questions:
- Describe a time when you significantly contributed to improving morale and productivity on your team. What were the underlying problems and their causes? How did you prevent them from negatively impacting the team in the future?
- What three things are you working on to improve your overall effectiveness?
- Give an example of a tough or critical piece of feedback you received. What was it and what did you do about it?
- Give me an example of an idea you had that was strongly opposed. Why was there so much resistance? How did you handle the negative feedback?
- Give me an example of a significant professional failure. What led you to making the wrong decision? What did you learn?
- Give an example of a time where you were not able to meet a commitment to a team member. What was the commitment and what prevented you from meeting it? What was the outcome and what did you learn?
- Building trust can be difficult. Tell me about how you have effectively built trusting working relationships with others on your team.
- Describe a time when you needed the cooperation of a peer who was resistant. What did you do? What was the outcome?
- Tell me about a piece of direct feedback you recently gave to a colleague. How did they respond? How do you like to receive feedback from others?
- Tell me about a time you had to communicate a big change in direction for which you anticipated people would have concerns. How did you handle questions and resistance?
- Tell me about a time your team's goals were out of alignment with another team on which you relied for a key resource. How did you work with the other team? Were you able to achieve your goals? (Manager)
- Tell me about a time you uncovered a significant problem in your team. What was it and how did you communicate it to your manager and other stakeholders? (Manager)

## Sample notes (from the bank, for style)

Demonstrated ownership across two examples: ..."), then Concern (specific gaps, e.g. limited partner-facing experience, thin technical depth).

**In-the-moment notes:** "Ownership Q1: <question>" followed by abbreviated STAR (client, what broke, what the candidate did, how long, outcome) and probes. Capture numbers and named systems as they are said.
