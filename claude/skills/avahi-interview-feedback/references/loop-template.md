# Loop (ORBIT) interview template

Write in first person, in my voice ("I asked", "I followed up", "I could not validate"). Never write "the interviewer".

A loop session is a behavioral interview on one or more ORBIT values. Avahi's BambooHR guidance: cover each ORBIT value assessed, include specific candidate examples in abbreviated STAR format, close with a recommendation. The format below keeps that and adds the ratings and evidence rules from SKILL.md.

During the interview I capture notes per question (value, question number, abbreviated STAR, follow-up probes). The write-up turns those notes plus the transcript into the structure below. It mirrors the format I use for my own BambooHR write-ups, so keep the layout exactly: plain text, dashed dividers, `[ Q1 ]` markers, no bullets, no bold.

```
SUMMARY

<Paragraph 1: who the candidate is (role, years, background, current scope), then the inclination and the ratings, for example "I am inclined to proceed. They were at bar on both values assessed, Ownership and Band Member.">

<Paragraph 2: the strongest value and the weaker one, each with the reason and a concrete example. Say if depth was below what the level expects even when the candidate was articulate.>

<Optional paragraph: anything extra I ran (for example a simulated discovery call), process issues (language, nerves, connection, time), role fit.>

Recommendation: <my own wording, for example Hire, Proceed, Do not proceed>

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DETAILED FEEDBACK

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

<VALUE NAME> — <AT BAR / ABOVE BAR / BELOW BAR / INCONCLUSIVE>

[ Q1 ] "<Question as asked, from the bank or adapted to a CV project.>"

Situation and Task: <One or two sentences. Name the client, project, team size, stakes. For a general-approach question: "General approach, asked before an example.">
Action: <What the candidate personally did. Separate "I" from "we". Note if a follow-up was needed to get to personal actions.>
Result: <Outcome, with the metric or concrete consequence if given. Note if no result was offered.>
Follow-ups: <Probes I asked and what the candidate answered. Omit if none.>
Observation: <One plain verdict: ownership and depth versus the level, and versus the CV claim. Name what kept the section from the next rating up.>

[ Q2 ] "<Follow-up question that became its own answer, for example "Give me an example.">"

<Use only the labels that apply. A Q block can be just Action and Observation.>

[ Supporting evidence: <short label> ] <One paragraph. Evidence for this value that came up outside a bank question (a different question, a side story, a stated mindset). Say if it was not a recalled specific event.>

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

<NEXT VALUE> — <RATING>

[ Q1 ] ...

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

OTHER OBSERVATIONS (not ORBIT values)

<Optional. Short labeled paragraphs: Communication, exercises I ran outside the values (for example a discovery call simulation, with gaps), Logistics. Not rated.>

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

Add CONCERNS or PERSONAL NOTE sections only if the summary and Observation lines cannot carry the point. End the file with the dashed divider.

## What to look for per value

Use the bank's own "what this looks like in practice" lists as the yardstick (details in orbit-question-bank.md).

- **Ownership:** acted outside their area, flagged a missed commitment early to stakeholders, long-term over short-term, stepped in for a peer, clean handoffs. Weak: "that was not my job", problems surfaced only after they became failures.
- **Results:** hit goals under constraints, adapted when the goal was wrong, persisted through obstacles, can say what they traded off. Weak: effort described without outcome, outcome without metric.
- **Band Member:** shares credit, helps when their own plate is full, disagrees and then commits, promotes teammates. Weak: every example is a solo win, credit taken, conflict blamed on others.
- **Innovation:** questioned a process, learned a skill proactively and applied it, pushed a change through resistance. Weak: novelty with no adoption or outcome, learning that never changed how they work.
- **Trust:** owns failure candidly, receives and gives direct feedback, resets expectations early, builds working relationships. Weak: polished failure stories where the candidate was not at fault, no real feedback received.
- **Manager candidates:** also expect coaching, setting team goals, removing barriers, creating safety to take risks. Use the (Manager) questions.

## Writing rules specific to loop notes

- Keep STAR labels short. If the candidate did not give a Result, write "No result offered, I asked in follow-up: <answer>".
- Distinguish what the candidate did from what the team did. This is the most common reason an answer is below bar.
- If the same example is reused across values, say so.
- Do not rate a value the candidate was not asked about, even if an example touched it in passing. Material that touched another value goes in a "Supporting evidence" paragraph or in OTHER OBSERVATIONS.
- Each Observation says what held the section at its rating (for example "the result was qualitative only and the case was low stakes").
- Keep any context from my notes that I added myself (for example a comment from another interviewer and my agreement), in first person.
